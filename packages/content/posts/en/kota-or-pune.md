Kota gives a student a full-time JEE routine, away from home. A Kota programme in Pune gives the same kind of teaching while the student lives at home and stays in school. The real difference is not the teaching. It is hostel life, school, the costs outside the fee, and who is around your child at 16.

## A week in Kota

Your child lives in a hostel or a paying-guest room, often with a roommate they did not choose. Coaching classes take a large part of the day, and the rest goes to self-study, tests and getting through daily chores. Meals come from a mess. Calls home happen when there is time.

For some students this works well. Everything in their week points at one exam, and they like that. For others, the same week is lonely. Nobody at home sees a bad week coming, because nobody is at home.

## A week in Pune

Your child goes to school in the morning, eats at home, and comes to class. With us, classes run {{fact:batch.days}}, from {{fact:batch.timings#start}} to {{fact:batch.timings#end}}. Dinner is at home. Self-study happens at a desk you can see.

The routine is still demanding. JEE preparation takes the same hours wherever it happens. But the student keeps their school, their friends and their family around them while they do it.

## What stays the same

What makes Kota coaching work is not the city. It is the teaching: experienced teachers who have taken batch after batch through the same syllabus, and a calendar of tests that does not let a chapter slide.

Our JEE programme is powered by a top coaching institute in Kota, and faculty from Kota teach it here in Pune. [Powered by Kota](page:powered-by-kota) explains what comes from Kota and what we do ourselves.

## The costs outside the fee

When families compare Kota and Pune, they often compare coaching fees alone. Kota adds costs that never appear on the fee receipt:

- Hostel or paying-guest rent, for two years.
- Mess or food bills.
- Travel home for festivals and holidays, and for parents to visit.
- A phone and data plan good enough for long calls home.

In Pune, the main extra is the trip to class, and you can measure that before you decide.

## Sixteen, and on their own

A Class 11 student in Kota is usually 15 or 16. They manage money, food, sleep and study on their own, for the first time, under exam pressure.

Some students grow up fast and do well. Some struggle and do not say so. Before choosing Kota, ask yourself honestly which of these your child is likely to be, and how quickly you would know if things went wrong.

## When Kota may still be the right choice

Kota can make sense when:

- the student wants it, strongly, and has thought about hostel life;
- home is a hard place to study, and that will not change;
- there is no programme near home with teachers of the same experience.

If none of these is true, a Kota programme in your own city gives your child the teaching without the cost of leaving.

## Questions to ask yourself

- Does my child study better with people around, or alone?
- Who will notice if they have a bad month?
- Have we added up the costs outside the fee?
- Have we seen what is on offer near home?
- Can we carry two years of costs outside the fee?

It also helps to talk to two families: one whose child went to Kota, and one whose child stayed home. Ask both what they would do again.
