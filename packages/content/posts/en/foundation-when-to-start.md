An IIT foundation course in Class 9 is worth it only if it makes school Maths and Science stronger and builds good study habits. It must not take over your child's life. It is not worth it if it rushes the JEE syllabus, crowds out school, or costs your child sleep and play. The age rules for coaching matter too.

## What a foundation programme should do

**Make school Maths and Science solid.** Class 9 and 10 Maths and Science are the ground everything in JEE stands on. A good foundation programme makes sure your child truly understands them, not just scores well in them. Algebra, geometry, motion, atoms and chemical reactions should be clear, not half-remembered.

**Build the habit of solving problems.** JEE rewards students who enjoy a hard problem. A foundation course can grow that, with puzzles and questions a little beyond the school book.

**Teach steady work.** Regular practice, short tests and asking for help early are habits that pay off for years. Class 9 is a good time to start them, when the stakes are low.

**Keep curiosity alive.** A student who still finds Physics interesting at 16 has an easier two years ahead than one who is already tired of it.

## What it should never do

**Rush the JEE syllabus.** Teaching Class 11 chapters to a Class 9 student is not a head start. It usually leaves gaps that show up later.

**Crowd out school.** The Class 10 board exam still matters. A foundation programme that makes school feel unimportant is doing harm.

**Take the whole week.** Children in Class 9 need sleep, sport, friends and free time. A programme that fills every evening will burn out the very student it is meant to help.

## The age rules

In 2024 the Ministry of Education issued guidelines for coaching centres, including rules on the age at which students can be enrolled. Before you sign up for any foundation course, ask the centre how it follows these rules.

## Questions to ask a foundation programme

- How many hours a week, and on which days?
- Does it follow the school syllabus, the JEE syllabus, or both?
- How does it fit around the Class 10 board exam?
- What does a test look like, and what happens after it?
- How does it follow the 2024 guidelines on age?

## Signs it is not working

- Your child dreads going.
- School marks start to fall.
- Sleep gets shorter every month.
- They can repeat answers but cannot explain them.

If you see these, step back. A foundation year that leaves a child tired of Science has cost more than it gave.

## Is your child ready?

A foundation programme suits a child who:

- enjoys Maths and Science, and asks "why";
- copes well with school and has time to spare;
- wants to do it, rather than being sent.

If your child is struggling with school Maths, the better step is help with school Maths first. Foundation can come later, or not at all. Plenty of students start JEE preparation in Class 11 with no foundation course and do well.

## Talk to the school too

Your child's Maths and Science teachers see them every day. Ask them two questions. Does my child understand the work, or only finish it? Do they ask questions in class? The answers tell you more about readiness than any entrance test for a foundation course.
