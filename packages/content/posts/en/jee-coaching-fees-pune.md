Compare JEE coaching fees by what you get for them, not by the headline figure. Write down what each fee includes, add the costs outside it, and divide by the hours of teaching. Two fees that look far apart often come close once both include the same things.

## What a JEE coaching fee should include

Before comparing, ask each centre for a written list of what the fee covers. A complete fee for a JEE programme usually includes:

- **Teaching** for the full course, in all three subjects.
- **Study material**: notes, solved examples and practice problems for every chapter.
- **Tests**: chapter tests, regular mock tests and full-length JEE mocks.
- **Test analysis**: a report after each test that shows what to work on.
- **Doubt support**: a set time, place or forum for questions outside class.
- **Online access**: recorded lectures for missed classes, if the centre has them.

If any of these is charged separately, add it to the fee before you compare.

## Costs outside the fee

Some costs never appear on the fee receipt:

- Travel to class and back, every class day, for one or two years.
- Reference books beyond the material the centre gives.
- A separate test series, if the centre's own tests are thin.
- A device and data for online tests and lectures.
- Taxes, if the quoted figure does not already include them. Ask.

## What Kota adds

If you are weighing Kota against Pune, the coaching fee is only the start. Kota adds hostel or paying-guest rent, food, travel home, and visits from parents, for two years. Our guide on [Kota or Pune](page:post-kota-or-pune) goes through these in more detail.

## Compare by the hour

Here is a simple way to put two quotes side by side. Ask each centre how many hours of class a week, for how many weeks. Multiply them to get the total hours of teaching. Then divide the full fee by those hours.

For example, a programme with classes four hours a day, six days a week, gives 24 hours of teaching each week. Over a year, that adds up to a lot of teaching. A cheaper course with half the hours can cost more per hour of teaching.

Do the same with the number of full mock tests in the course. A programme with a mock every month gives a student many more chances to practise the real exam than one with a handful in the final term.

This is not the only measure. A good teacher's hour is worth more than an average one. But it stops a low headline figure from hiding a thin course.

## Questions to ask about the fee

- Is this the fee for the full course, or for one year?
- Does it include study material, the test series and online lectures?
- Are taxes included?
- Is there a registration or admission charge on top?
- How is it paid, and on which dates?
- What is refunded if my child leaves, and how soon?

## Cheaper is not always cheaper

A low fee can hide a course with large batches, few tests and no doubt support. That often ends with a second coaching class or a separate test series in Class 12. Then the family pays twice, and the student loses months switching.

## Payment and refunds

Ask how the fee is paid: in one go, or in parts. Ask what happens if your child leaves the course early, and get the answer in writing before you pay. A centre with fair rules will not mind putting them on paper.
