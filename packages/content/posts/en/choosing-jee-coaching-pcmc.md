A centre visit tells you more than any brochure. When you visit a JEE coaching centre in Pimpri-Chinchwad, do four things. Look at a real classroom and ask to see a real test report. Talk to a parent whose child already studies there, and make the trip at class time.

## Before you go

Fix the visit for a weekday, during class hours. A centre on a Sunday morning is an empty room. A centre at 4 PM on a Tuesday shows you how it actually runs.

Take your child with you. They are the one who will spend two years there, and they will notice things you do not.

## In the classroom

Ask to stand at the back of a class for ten minutes. Then look at three things.

**Can every student see the board?** Count the rows. A student in the last row of a long hall copies what the student in front of them wrote, not what the teacher said.

**Does the teacher ask questions, or only answer them?** Good JEE teaching is full of short questions to the room. A class where only the teacher talks is a lecture, and a lecture can be watched at home.

**What is on the board?** Look for worked problems, not only theory. JEE is a problem-solving exam.

## Ask to see a test report

Ask for a sample report from a recent test, with the name hidden. A useful report shows marks by subject and by chapter, and points to what to work on next. A report that shows only a total and a rank tells a student where they stand, but not what to do.

Then ask who reads the report with the student. A report nobody discusses gets filed and forgotten.

## Talk to a parent who is already there

Ask the centre to put you in touch with a current parent, or speak to one at the gate. Ask them two things: what surprised them after joining, and what they would change. Their answers are worth more than anything on the walls.

## Check the safety basics

Look at who is at the entrance, how students sign in and out, and how the centre would reach you if your child did not arrive. For a student coming alone in the evening, these details matter.

## Make the trip at class time

Pimpri-Chinchwad is spread out, and the roads change by the hour. Make the trip from home at the time your child would travel, and back at the time class ends. From Wakad or [Pimple Saudagar](page:area-pimple-saudagar), a centre in the same part of town can save your child a long crossing twice a day.

## Questions to ask before you leave

- How many students are in this batch, and will that number hold all year?
- Who teaches each subject, and how long have they been at this centre?
- How often are full mock tests held, and are they on a screen or on paper?
- Where does a student take a doubt after class?
- What happens if my child misses a class?
- What is the full fee, and what are the refund rules? Can I have both in writing?

## After the visit

Write down the answers the same day, before the details blur. If you visit two centres, compare them on the same list. Then ask your child which room they could see themselves working in for two years. Their answer matters as much as yours.
